For sites with no smartphone, Pehra is a box.

About ₹600 of parts around an ESP32. It measures the real WBGT on the site, acts on it, and talks to AWS IoT Core. Every box gets a live 3D twin.

DHT22

Air temperature and humidity: the wet-bulb part of WBGT.

Black globe probe

Radiant heat from the sun, tin roofs and concrete: the globe part.

Two sonar sensors

Drinking-water tank level and drain level: refill, and waterlogging alerts.

Ring, siren, relays, SOS

Risk colour, a rest bell every hour, a mist fan, a tank pump, and a red button that calls the supervisor.

How it runs

  1. Forecast to WBGT, once per city. EventBridge runs a Lambda three times a day; Open-Meteo in, WBGT out to S3. A million sites cost the same as one.
  2. Plan, approve, send. WhatsApp hits API Gateway and a Lambda; Step Functions waits up to 12 hours for the supervisor's Approve; SQS rate-limits the sends; Polly speaks the Hindi.
  3. AI explains, policy guards. Amazon Bedrock with Strands Agents writes the explanation; every number comes from tools; Cedar makes sure the AI can never loosen a limit.
  4. Devices and records. IoT Core takes the Site Box readings over X.509 MQTT; DynamoDB holds sites, people and boxes; each day's record is hash-chained in S3 Object Lock.

One SAM stack in ap-south-1 (Mumbai). Source on GitHub, 246 tests.

What Pehra does not claim

It does not save lives and it is not medical advice. It is designed to reduce exposure. Every number it sends comes from code, never from the model.
